Five charities

Fixed in the contract when it was deployed. There is no function to add a sixth, remove one, or change where any of them point. Adding a charity would mean deploying a different launchpad, and you'd be able to tell.

Payouts route through donate.gg, which holds the relationship with each charity and resolves its current payout address. Each config ID below is a public value published on that charity's page.

How it works

  1. You launch a coin and pick a charity

    The launchpad deploys a vault for your campaign and launches the token through pons, with the vault set as the creator fee recipient. Whatever recipient you pass is discarded and replaced — a launcher cannot aim fees at themselves.

  2. Trading generates creator fees

    Fees are swept from the bonding curve into the campaign's vault, in ETH. Anyone can trigger the next step; nothing depends on us running a bot.

  3. The vault bridges to the charity

    The vault's only outbound call sends ETH to Ethereum, where a forwarder donates it under the charity's config. Every hop is a public transaction you can follow.

Why it can't be redirected

What we can't promise

A page like this usually says "trustless" and stops. Here is the specific, complete list of what remains outside our control, so you can price it yourself.

Two parties retain power. Neither can act quietly.

The pons protocol owner can move a token's fee recipient, but only by proposing the change publicly and waiting out a 3-day timelock. And donate.gg, whose relay receives the final donation, can upgrade that contract — it's a proxy under one key.

Payouts take about six days. Sometimes seconds.

The route that always works is the chain's own bridge, and it carries a 6.36-day challenge period — the delay every optimistic rollup withdrawal has. It depends on nothing but the rollup, anyone can trigger it, and it cannot be blocked.

There is also a fast route through Across that settles in seconds for a few hundredths of a percent, and the contract caps what any caller can concede to relayers so nobody can quote a hostile rate and keep the difference. But it depends on third-party relayers keeping capital on this route, and on a chain this young that comes and goes — we have seen it quote seven seconds in the morning and offer nothing by the evening.

So treat the fast route as a bonus and the six days as the real answer. A donation is never stuck either way: the slow route is always available to anybody who wants to push it through.

"100% to the cause" would be false.

We take 0% — there is no fee parameter in our contracts and the donation tip is hardcoded to zero. But the pons protocol takes its own cut of trading fees before the creator share exists, as it does for every token on this chain. So: 100% of the creator fee share reaches the charity, not 100% of every trade.

Not audited yet.

The contracts compile and pass their tests. Nobody outside has reviewed them. Until that changes, treat this as software that has not been proven, because it hasn't.
